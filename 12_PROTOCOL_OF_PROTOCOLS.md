# 12 — Protocol of Protocols (Recurring Mechanisms)

Cross-cutting mechanisms that appear across many effective public protocols. Useful for product architecture: build engines, not only named rituals.

---

## 1. Exhale-biased / slow paced breathing

**Seen in:** cyclic sighing, resonance/~6 bpm, diaphragmatic, exhale-emphasized breathing, parts of TIPP/PMR cool-downs.  
**Claim level:** Moderate evidence that slow breathing and exhale emphasis reduce subjective stress and can raise RSA/HRV during practice.  
**Product implication:** Default breath engine = slow + longer exhale; holds optional advanced mode with contraindications.

---

## 2. Attention redirection / competing resources

**Seen in:** 5-4-3-2-1, body scan, mindfulness of breath, affect labeling, ART nature breaks, PPRs.  
**Claim level:** Strong cognitive principle; specific branded mnemonics vary in evidence.  
**Product implication:** Offer multiple attention anchors (sensory, breath, external task cue).

---

## 3. Stimulus control / cue–behavior binding

**Seen in:** CBT-I stimulus control, postpone worry, implementation intentions, habit stacking, Pomodoro timer as cue.  
**Claim level:** Strong for sleep stimulus control and if–then plans; moderate/practical for habit cues.  
**Product implication:** Always ask: what is the cue? what is the response? what context is incompatible?

---

## 4. Arousal titration (up or down) before cognition

**Seen in:** TIPP, cold face, exercise, centering breath, tactical breathing.  
**Claim level:** Clinically sensible; physiology plausible; micro-skill RCTs thinner than packages.  
**Product implication:** Gate “think differently” skills behind “body intensity check.”

---

## 5. Reconstrual / psychological distance

**Seen in:** cognitive reappraisal, self-distancing, mental contrasting (WOOP), ACT defusion (relationship-to-thought).  
**Claim level:** Strong for reappraisal/CBT; good experimental support for distancing/MCII; ACT package-level.  
**Product implication:** Separate tools that change content vs change relationship to thoughts vs contrast future/obstacle.

---

## 6. Behavioral approach / activation

**Seen in:** BA tiny steps, opposite action (when fear unjustified), exercise, social micro-connection, exposure-like opposite actions.  
**Claim level:** Strong for BA/depression; exercise strong for mood symptoms.  
**Product implication:** Every regulation path should end in a valued micro-action when safe.

---

## 7. Circadian zeitgebers

**Seen in:** morning light, evening dim, fixed wake, jet-lag timing, melatonin timing.  
**Claim level:** Strong basic science + clinical chronotherapy parameters.  
**Product implication:** Time-of-day aware recommendations beat generic “do breathwork.”

---

## 8. Functional equivalence / simulation quality

**Seen in:** PETTLEP, process visualization, WOOP outcome imagery (brief) + obstacle.  
**Claim level:** Moderate for PETTLEP/imagery; strong warning against outcome-only fantasy.  
**Product implication:** Imagery builder checklist > “visualize success” button.

---

## 9. Interoceptive learning (tension ↔ release)

**Seen in:** PMR, autogenic, body scan.  
**Claim level:** Moderate for PMR/relaxation training.  
**Product implication:** Teach discrimination, not only “relax harder.”

---

## 10. Rest ≠ sleep replacement

**Seen in:** Yoga Nidra/NSDR, quiet rest, meditation sleepiness.  
**Claim level:** Preliminary benefits for rest/sleep *quality subjective metrics*; **no strong evidence these replace sleep homeostasis/physiological functions of nocturnal sleep.**  
**Product implication:** Market as recovery/rest tools; never as sleep substitutes.

---

## Design rule of thumb

**State tools** (seconds–minutes) change now.  
**Trait/load tools** (days–weeks) change capacity (sleep, exercise, light, BA, CBT-I).  
Products that only ship state tools will under-deliver on chronic goals.
