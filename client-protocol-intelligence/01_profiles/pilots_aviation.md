## One-liner
FTL/FRMS and CRM dominate — layover circadian recovery and off-duty downshifts; never wellness over fitness-for-duty.

## Who
Commercial and business-jet pilots, cabin crew leads, aviation ops using public ICAO/EASA fatigue concepts.

## Constraints
- FTL / FRMS regulatory limits
- Controlled rest only per approved ops manual
- Medical certification / AME pathways
- No impairment — report unfit when needed

## Dominant stressors
- Fatigue and circadian misalignment
- Vigilance under monotony + spike
- Chronic jet lag
- CRM interpersonal load
- Layover sleep quality / owner pressure (bizav)

## Primary jobs-to-be-done
- Maximize layover recovery sleep
- Off-duty autonomic downshift without substituting for sleep
- Assert fitness-for-duty / FTL boundaries (esp. bizav)
- Support CRM clarity when fatigued risk rises — via policy not heroics

## Commercial notes
Low direct luxury (regulated); bizav UHNW owner-education adjacency High ethics value.

## Privacy / discretion
Rosters and medical cert sensitive; no public fatigue admissions coerced.

## Lifestyle rails (non-negotiable before exotic tools)
- Comply with FTL/FRMS / ops manual
- Protect uninterrupted sleep opportunity on layover
- Light timing for next FDP direction
- Caffeine strategy early in duty not late

## Hard avoids / contraindications (product)
- Breathing away microsleep risk
- Experimental breath holds in cockpit
- Unapproved controlled rest
- Alcohol as layover sleep aid
- Violating FTL because owner wants wheels-up
- NSDR as FDP recovery substitute for sleep

## Evidence posture for this type
ICAO Doc 9966 / EASA FTL-FRM frameworks A (regulatory science); naps/controlled rest B operational; jet-lag light B; off-duty coherent breathing B — never in lieu of sleep or fitness-for-duty.
