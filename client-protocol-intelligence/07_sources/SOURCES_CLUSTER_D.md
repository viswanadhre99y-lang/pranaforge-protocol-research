## Travel medicine / circadian / jet lag (cite carefully)

1. Riedy SM, Williams SG. Jet Lag Disorder. In: CDC Yellow Book, 2026 edition (published 2025-04-23). NCBI Bookshelf: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK620865/ · CDC page: https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/travel-air-sea/jet-lag-disorder.html  
   **Key cautions used:** Timed light primary; melatonin timing by internal clock (east advance / west delay); **0.5–1 mg often sufficient**; **avoid >5 mg** (residual melatonin wrong time); use jet-lag calculator; wrong-timed light worsens misalignment; caffeine as scheduling tool with cutoff.

2. Bin YS, et al. Interventions to Minimize Jet Lag After Westward and Eastward Flight. *Frontiers in Physiology*. 2019. https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2019.00927

3. AASM. Practice Parameters / Clinical Guidance materials for circadian rhythm sleep-wake disorders (historical practice parameters and 2015 intrinsic CRSWD guideline update). https://aasm.org/resources/practiceparameters/pp_circadianrhythm.pdf · Auger et al. J Clin Sleep Med 2015 PMC4582061: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4582061/

4. Morgenthaler TI, et al. Practice Parameters for the Clinical Evaluation and Treatment of Circadian Rhythm Sleep Disorders. *SLEEP*. 2007. (Includes jet lag / shift work parameter language in AASM tradition.)

5. CDC Yellow Book — Complementary and Integrative Health Approaches (melatonin product quality / contamination notes). https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/preparing-international-travelers/complementary-and-integrative-health-approaches.html

6. Human Kinetics / IJSNEM travel nutrition reviews (meal timing as secondary cue; limited monotherapy; weak crash-fast evidence). Example context: https://journals.humankinetics.com/view/journals/ijsnem/29/2/article-p228.xml
